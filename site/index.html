What you ask for

Just name the topic

The skill picks the scope from your request, says it in one line, and gets to work in ./courses/.

"Plan a System Design course"

A course plan

Outcomes, prerequisites and chapters in dependency order, each with one planned figure. Built into a syllabus page and a catalog.

"Write the binary search chapter"

A full chapter

A fixed teaching arc from hook to recap, code that has been run, its signature figure, and a built page.

"Notes on consistent hashing"

Notes and interview prep

A compressed arc: idea, mechanism, code, pitfalls, two checks. Reuses a chapter figure where one fits.

"Cheat sheet for graphs"

A cheat sheet

Tables, patterns, complexity grids and one-liners on one or two screens. No new figures, links for depth.

"A course on how rockets work"

"Lesson on how the heart pumps"

From school students to professionals. The writing adapts; the checks stay the same.

How it works

Text and figure share one source of truth

The figure is driven by the same model as the chapter's worked example, so the numbers on the card are the numbers in the text.

  1. Outline

    One outcome sentence per section, then the single insight that most needs to be seen moving.

  2. Brief the figure

    The idea, the truth model with the chapter's exact numbers, a physical object from an atlas of about 90 concepts, and the tour beats.

  3. Build it

    The Anatomy engine draws a detailed isometric machine with a live readout, then audits it for painter's order and clipping.

  4. Write and check

    The chapter is written around the figure, every code block is run, and the page is checked on desktop, phone and dark mode.

Quality bar

Checked, not just generated

  • Every code block is run, every number verified, and real-world facts taken from published sources, not memory.
  • Pages pass a headless check: no JS errors, clipped or missing figures, broken links or sideways scroll.
  • Figures pass their own audit and are reviewed at 4x, on a 390px phone and in both themes.
  • One house style: dense prose, no filler, no em dashes. Builds warn and checks catch anything that slips.
  • Markdown is the source, with GitHub-style callouts and a one-line ::figure directive for your own platform.
  • Pages are self-contained HTML with figures embedded, in light and dark.

Requirements

One install, a few local tools

The Anatomy figure engine is bundled, so the skill is all you add. Building pages and figures uses tools you likely have.
